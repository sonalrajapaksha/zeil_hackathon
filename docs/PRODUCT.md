# Product truth — Access

**Positioning:** Access is an accessibility-first AI career companion that removes barriers in traditional job applications. It is designed with people who face accessibility challenges in mind and is available to everyone.

Access is not a disability assessment tool, hiring decision system, employability scorer, or product restricted to disabled candidates. It does not ask for diagnoses or infer disability or medical conditions.

**Promise:** The application adapts to the candidate, rather than forcing the candidate through inaccessible forms. Candidates choose how they want to communicate and control every profile claim and disclosure.

**Audience:** Anyone seeking career assistance, including people who face barriers with conventional application forms and people who simply prefer conversational, flexible support. Do not assume a disability type, diagnose, or infer an access need from a person's behavior.

**Core job-to-be-done:** Describe experience conversationally; choose simple or standard interview questions; use text (always available); review evidence-backed profile proposals; approve, edit, or remove them; select a job; create an accessible editable CV and cover letter; export with control.

**Communication and access preferences:** Ask the candidate directly. Display preferences include high contrast, large text, and reduced motion. Question style is simple or standard and can be changed by the candidate. The interviewer asks one question at a time, allows skipping and clarification, and does not treat a clarification request as career evidence. Voice interaction is optional and only exists when explicitly implemented; it never replaces the complete text flow.

**Key experience:** Conversation + a growing Living Career Canvas. Newly extracted claims remain unconfirmed, show where they came from, and require candidate action before use. Sensitive information is never automatically disclosed in CVs or cover letters, even if mentioned in conversation.

**North-star demo:** A fictional jobseeker finds multi-step application forms difficult to navigate and chooses simple questions, large text, and keyboard-operated text chat. Access asks one question at a time; the candidate skips or clarifies when useful, then reviews an evidence-backed suggestion, edits and approves it, chooses a clearly fictional customer-support role, reviews the editable draft, and downloads it. The narrative demonstrates barrier removal without requiring a diagnosis or claiming the product is only for disabled people. No actual application submission.

**Success:** A candidate can complete the core journey with keyboard and screen reader support, at their chosen question style and display preferences, while controlling every disclosure. The demo fits within three minutes after setup. Report only observed test results; do not claim WCAG compliance without verification.
